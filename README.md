# Limited Intelligence (Intelligenza Limitata)

A theoretical framework for **bounded intelligence** and **systemic coherence**, modeling limits as a proportional equilibrium between:

- **E** — Energy (cost, compute, metabolic load)
- **I** — Information (throughput, noise, uncertainty)
- **S** — Structure (complexity, coupling, fragility)
- **V** — Functional Value (capability, outcome value, legitimacy)

---

## Core idea

Coherence holds only under **bounded growth**.  
When scaling exceeds proportional constraints, efficiency drops, signal degrades, and reasoning fractures into noise.

This framework treats limits not as failures, but as **structural safeguards** of intelligence and legitimacy.

---

## Conceptual formulation (non-empirical, non-clinical)

A compact conceptual expression:

\[
C = \frac{V}{E + I + S}
\]

Where higher **C** indicates higher coherence per unit burden.

This expression is **conceptual and comparative**, not a quantitative or predictive model.

Examples Overview
Each example is documented in its own markdown file under the /examples directory.
This repository includes a set of cross-domain conceptual examples illustrating how the Congruity / Limited Intelligence framework applies to different complex systems.
These examples are interpretative case studies, not implementations, and are meant to show how apparent contradictions, anomalies, or failures often emerge from structural mismatches across scale, cost, time, and functional value, rather than from local errors.
Current example domains include:
Physics
Hubble Tension: divergence in the measured expansion rate of the universe across observational regimes, interpreted as a coherence issue between scales and measurement horizons.
Finance
Market Dislocations: extreme price movements analyzed as losses of proportionality across leverage, liquidity, informational velocity, and time horizons.
AI Systems
Conceptual analyses of system behavior under bounded / limited intelligence, where local optimization degrades global coherence.
Biology
Illustrative examples of constrained growth, saturation, and functional degradation under scaling pressure.
Governance
Structural framing of why constraint-aware intelligence is necessary for legitimacy, safety, and long-term stability.
Each example follows the same principle:
the goal is diagnosis, not prediction.
Why Scale Mismatches Matter
Across physics, finance, biology, and intelligent systems, a recurring pattern emerges:
Systems tend to fail not because they are wrong, but because they grow out of proportion.
When scale increases faster than the system’s ability to:
absorb information,
distribute cost,
synchronize time horizons,
or preserve functional value,
coherence degrades.
This often manifests as:
tensions between measurement regimes (physics),
sudden volatility releases (finance),
fragility and collapse (biology),
or misaligned, unsafe optimization (AI systems).
Within the Congruity / Limited Intelligence framework, these phenomena are treated as structural outcomes, not anomalies:
signals appear late,
feedback is delayed,
and instability accumulates silently before release.
The framework therefore shifts the focus:
from forecasting outcomes
to identifying proportional limits and coherence thresholds.
This perspective is essential for:
understanding systemic risk,
designing constraint-aware intelligence,
and grounding governance in structural constraints rather than reactive control mechanisms.
