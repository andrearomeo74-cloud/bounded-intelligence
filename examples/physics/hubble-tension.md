# Hubble Tension — Congruity Case Study

## Problem
The Hubble Constant (H) defines the present expansion rate of the universe:

v = H × D

Two robust measurement pipelines disagree:
- **Early Universe (CMB + ΛCDM):** ~67–68 km/s/Mpc
- **Late Universe (distance ladder):** ~73–74 km/s/Mpc

This discrepancy is known as the *Hubble Tension*.

## Congruity interpretation
Within the Congruity / Limited Intelligence framework, this is not necessarily a contradiction,
but a **loss of coherence across scale and regime**.

A single scalar parameter is forced to remain invariant across:
- different observational scales
- different informational pipelines
- different structural assumptions

When proportionality breaks, tension emerges.

## Minimal congruent formulation (conceptual)
We define an effective parameter:

H_eff = V / (E + I + S)

Where:
- **V** = functional value of the estimate
- **E** = energetic and observational cost
- **I** = informational uncertainty and noise
- **S** = structural complexity and coupling

Different regimes naturally produce different H_eff values.

## Interpretation
- Early-universe inference maximizes global coherence but depends on strong model structure.
- Late-universe measurements are locally grounded but accumulate systematics and coupling noise.

The “tension” can therefore be read as **regime-dependent congruity**, not immediate model failure.

models remain locally correct but lose global coherence when pushed beyond proportional limits.
